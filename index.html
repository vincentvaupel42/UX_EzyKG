    The problem today

    Matching a paper diary to a screen is slow work.

    A 12 lead Holter monitor is worn for 3 to 4 days. The patient writes symptoms and activities in a paper diary. Afterwards the doctor reviews the ECG in a desktop application and has to find each diary entry in the recording.

    TopicHolter and paper diaryEzyKG
    Wearing it A bulky recorder with wires on the chest. One small chest patch that records ECG, motion and rotation.
    Patient notes Written by hand in a paper diary. Tapped into a phone app and timestamped automatically.
    Finding the moment The doctor reads the diary, then searches the recording for each entry. Notes sit on the ECG timeline at the minute they were made.
    Activity Only what the patient remembered to write down. Activity and body position come from the motion sensors, so rest and movement show without a note.
    Unnoticed events Missed unless the patient felt something and wrote it down. Findings such as irregular pulse or high pulse on exertion are calculated automatically, also where the patient wrote nothing.
    Device status Hard for the patient to tell whether it is recording. The app shows whether the patch is connected and recording.

    How it works

    From the patient's chest to the doctor's screen.

    1. 1

      Wear

      A light patch records ECG, motion and rotation, with a timestamp on every sample.

    2. 2

      Note

      The patient's phone receives the stream over Bluetooth. Symptoms and activities are tapped in as they happen, and short check ins ask for more detail.

    3. 3

      Sync

      The phone sends everything to a cloud service that stores the data and prepares it for review.

    4. 4

      Review

      The doctor picks a patient who is ready for review and gets an overview of the whole period. Patient notes and automatic findings sit on the same timeline, and one tap opens the ECG for that moment.

    What the doctor gets back

    Less matching, more diagnosing.

    EzyKG is built around a short list of things that matter to a heart doctor and to the patient wearing the patch.

    Functional

    Saves time
    The overview shows where to look, so nobody scrolls through 72 hours of ECG to find the minute a symptom happened.
    Organizes
    Patient notes, findings, activity and body position live on one timeline, and the ECG for the same time span is one tap away.
    Integrates
    ECG, motion and notes from three sources arrive as one record.
    Informs
    Findings point to rhythm changes that the patient did not write down, for example an irregular pulse mostly at night. The system points and the doctor decides.
    Reduces effort
    No diary pages to read and no handwriting to decipher.
    Avoids hassles
    A small patch replaces a bulky monitor with wires.

    Emotional

    Reduces anxiety
    Patients can see that the patch is working. Doctors see every finding listed with the times it happened.

    Who it is for

    Made for the people who read the recording.

    Heart doctors and their clinics

    Clinics that diagnose atrial fibrillation and review Holter data. If a handwritten diary has ever sat next to your screen while you scrolled for one minute of ECG, EzyKG was designed for that moment.

    Patients with possible atrial fibrillation

    Adults from 40 to 65 who use a smartphone regularly. The app asks for little and works the way other apps do, so notes get made when something happens.
